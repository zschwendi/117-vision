# Controls

Pair a compatible controller with Vision Pro before launching.

## PS VR2 Sense

Pair the left and right Sense controllers separately and allow accessory tracking.
Use immersive mode for tracked aiming. Move the right controller to position and
aim the weapon; move your head to look around. The left stick moves relative to
where you are looking. You can physically turn through 360° in every mode.

**Flashlight (Q): hold left Create and push the left stick up.**

### App controls without putting the controllers down

In the launcher and app settings, controller navigation reveals an outline on
the selected control; touch and gaze/pinch do not require that selector.
Move the **left stick up/down** to choose, **left/right** to adjust a value, and
press **Cross** to activate. **Circle** goes back. In the game menu, choose
**Back to Halo** or press Circle to close the native panel. If Halo itself is
paused, its original pause menu still needs to be resumed.

Pausing during gameplay reveals a small **Settings** button. It opens the game
menu, with view switching, live settings and **Quit Game**. Quit requires
confirmation; starting another session afterwards requires reopening the app.
The auxiliary settings panel does not open automatically on startup.

During play, release other buttons, center both sticks, then press **Create +
Options** together to reopen Halo Controls. This gives access to Recenter View,
weapon mode, window/immersive presentation and settings. Sense button and stick gameplay input,
turning and gestures are held while app controls own the controllers; Halo's
simulation keeps running. Release buttons and center the sticks after closing
the panel before gameplay input resumes. The native menu also holds ordinary
gamepad input; ordinary gameplay mappings are unchanged when it is closed.
Importing files and other system dialogs may still need system input.

### Turning

Choose a turning mode in Halo Controls or Sense Controls:

- **Point-to-turn + stick** (default): move the right weapon's reticle toward the
  left or right edge of your view and hold briefly to turn smoothly. Bring it
  back toward the center to stop. Using the right stick takes over with smooth
  turning and tilting.
- **Smooth stick**: push the right stick left or right for smooth turning, or up
  or down to tilt your view, while aiming freely.
- **Physical turning only**: turn your body; the right stick does not turn or
  tilt the view.
- **30° or 45° snap turns**: flick the right stick left or right for a fixed
  turn, then center it before the next turn. Up and down do not tilt the view.

In the full Sense Controls settings, **Stick speed** is saved from 30–120°/s
(default 90°/s). The right stick turns at up to twice that (default 180°/s) and
tilts the view up or down at up to that speed (default 90°/s), at most 45° either
way. Up and down tilt the direction you are currently looking and never change
which way the left stick moves you. Tilting moves the world,
both hands and the reticle together, and the world then stays put: if you
physically turn away afterwards, the tilted horizon can look rolled. Recenter View
levels it, and choosing physical or snap turning levels it too. Point-to-turn
turns at up to half the saved speed (default 45°/s).
**Point-turn boundary** sets how far from the center the reticle moves before
turning starts, from 50–90% of the distance between the center and either side
of your view (default 72%). A higher boundary leaves more room to aim without turning. These settings
are saved separately from the turning mode. Center the stick and aim after
changing modes or recovering tracking before turning resumes.

### Weapon modes

**Single weapon** is the default. Bring your left hand forward toward a rifle's
foregrip for two-handed support; move it away for one-handed aiming. The rifle
keeps its original length. Automatic support is enabled for the assault rifle,
sniper rifle, shotgun, plasma rifle, rocket launcher and flamethrower when the
original rig and animation are ready. Individual rig feel still needs headset
acceptance. The pistol remains one-handed. **Hold L1, make a throwing motion with
your left hand, then release L1**. While armed, the selected frag or plasma
grenade is shown in the left palm. This preview spends no grenade; releasing
L1 requests the original throw. The grenade starts at that hand's release
position and travels in the direction your hand was moving. Your hand speed sets
the strength against Halo's normal grenade throw: a 5 m/s throw or faster is a
full normal throw, 3 m/s is 60% and 1 m/s is 20%; it never exceeds the normal
throw, and releasing a still hand drops the grenade. The 5 m/s full-strength
point is a game-feel calibration, not a measured comfort limit. Halo's original throw animation still plays
first, so the grenade leaves a moment after L1 rises, from where the hand was at
release. Keep the controller tracked through release; tracking loss before
release, or before Halo creates the grenade, cancels the attempt without using a
grenade. Lowering or hiding your hand after release does not cancel the throw.
After release, dying, entering a vehicle or seat, or handing control to another
controller after Halo has created the actual grenade removes it without a
throw; that grenade is used. Cancelling only the armed preview spends nothing.
Halo keeps its original gravity, fuse, spin and collision, including stopping
the grenade at a wall between your eyes and your hand. Ordinary gamepad grenades
are unchanged.

Choose **Dual wielding** in Halo Controls or Sense Controls to use an assault
rifle and pistol from your inventory together. Each gun has its own aim and
ammunition. R2 fires the right gun and L2 fires the left; Square requests both
reloads. If a valid pair is unavailable, a supported rifle can still use the
left hand for support. L2 does nothing until an actual second gun is ready.
Arming a grenade temporarily replaces the left gun with the grenade preview.
The gun remains in inventory and returns after release; release L2 before firing
it again.

Press **R1** for melee. Halo's original melee animation temporarily takes over
the tracked weapon pose. Press **R3** to zoom: the scope stays centered on your
view and aiming follows your weapon, including two-handed support.
The primary weapon's reload and put-away/ready animations also take over for
their original duration, then return to tracked aiming. Arm gestures are held
during that takeover; stick turning remains available. A committed physical
magazine reload retains its tracked presentation, as does the dual offhand's
independent reload.

Sense has no D-pad or touchpad. The Create combinations use its real left stick;
movement is suspended while Create is held. Release the triggers after entering
tracked play or recovering tracking. Tracking loss suspends firing.

Tracked weapon support currently covers on-foot campaign play. In a campaign
turret or vehicle seat the Sense controllers work as an ordinary gamepad for
that seat: the sticks aim and move, R2 fires (release it after getting in) and
the game's own crosshair returns. The view recentres once on entering and on
leaving the seat, and tracked aiming resumes on foot. Switch off Tracked aiming
for multiplayer. These Sense-specific mappings leave ordinary controllers
unchanged.

### Quick gestures

**Quick gestures** is on by default and can be switched off in Sense Controls.
It works during tracked on-foot campaign play. Ordinary gamepads and all button
fallbacks remain available, including R1 melee, Triangle swap and Create utilities.

Start with your hands settled in front, release the triggers and buttons, and
leave the right stick centered. You may keep moving with the left stick. These
actions need **no Create button**, including while scoped:

- **Melee:** make a firm forward, sideways or downward swing with either hand.
  The other hand may stay on the rifle's foregrip. Halo performs its original
  melee animation, range check and damage; contact with a controller is not needed.
- **Switch equipped weapon:** reach your right hand beside your shoulder and
  pause briefly. The swap commits while the controller is still visible, before
  a reach farther behind your back could lose tracking. Bring the hand back in
  front to ready the next gesture.

Each motion triggers once. Return to a settled front-ready position before
another strike or swap; there is a short cooldown. Slow aim adjustments, small
jitter and head movement do not count. Losing tracking cancels an unfinished
motion. If tracking drops behind your back, return in front and try a visible
shoulder reach. Both controllers must remain tracked for gameplay gestures.

For these actions, **hold the left Create button** with both sticks centered
and the scope lowered:

- **Reload the right gun:** tap near its grip twice with your left hand, pulling
  away between taps. You do not need to hit the controllers together.
- **Pick up a weapon:** look at a nearby weapon and reach your left hand forward.
  Halo's normal pickup targeting and range still apply.

Each Create press permits one action. Release and press again to retry. These
attempts expire after three seconds; releasing Create cancels an unfinished
attempt, while a recognized action remains committed until delivered or invalidated
by tracking/ownership loss. Reload plays the normal animation and only reloads
the right gun; Square still requests both reloads in Dual wielding. Pickup does
not attach a world object to your hand. With gestures on, Create + R3 shows the
scoreboard without zooming; with gestures or tracked aiming off, centered Create
retains its normal scoreboard behavior.

### Real hands and controllers

**Show real hands & controllers** is on by default in Halo Controls and Sense
Controls. This asks visionOS to reveal your real hands and held Sense controllers
over the game. The setting updates while the immersive space is open.

If a controller remains hidden, check visionOS Settings → Awareness & Safety →
Reveal Game Controllers → Always. The system controls passthrough visibility.
Use Recenter View after changing your seated, standing or reclined position.

Apple documents the shared hands/accessories setting in
[Explore spatial accessory input on visionOS](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/289/)
and the system preference in
[See your controller while immersed](https://support.apple.com/guide/apple-vision-pro/tan476683e88/visionos).

The following uses PlayStation labels. Equivalent Xbox buttons follow the same
physical positions. These are the default PC keyboard/mouse bindings used by the
app; a custom Halo controller profile can assign different actions.

## Implementation notes

The saved weapon-mode preference starts in Single weapon even if build 111 had
automatically enabled its experimental dual-wield toggle. Later explicit mode
choices persist. Turning preferences now use version 2: the previous 30°/45° snap
choice migrates once to Point-to-turn + stick, while an explicit physical-only
choice is preserved. New profiles start with Point-to-turn + stick. Later choices,
including snap turning, persist without being migrated again. The old version 1
turning preference is retained. Tracked aiming, weapon mode, visibility and
ordinary gamepad settings are unchanged by this turning migration.
Quick gestures use their own saved preference and leave those choices intact.
No launch flag is required. `HALO_SENSE_WEAPON=0` remains an optional diagnostic
override.

The app uses the public `upperLimbVisibility` preference for hands and accessories;
it does not access passthrough camera images. Apple makes the final visibility
decision. Button mappings follow Apple's
[spatial-controller input definitions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/gamecontroller/discovering-and-tracking-spatial-game-controllers-and-styli).
